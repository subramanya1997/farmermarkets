A farmers market does not keep store hours. It exists for a few hours a week, on days chosen by the people who organize it, and the whole thing can pack up in November and reappear in May. That is why the question of when a market is open rarely has a tidy answer, and why the answer you find once may not hold six months later.

## Most Markets Run a Season

Across most of the country the outdoor season follows the growing season, opening somewhere around May or June and closing in October or November. Where winters are long the window is shorter, so a market in Vermont may run for a few months while one in Georgia runs most of the year. Warm-climate markets often operate year-round, and so do many indoor markets and public market halls, which have a roof and heat and no reason to stop.

## One or Two Fixed Days a Week

A typical market picks a weekday and keeps it. Every Wednesday, every Saturday, same spot, same hours. Larger markets sometimes run two days, and a single organization may operate several locations on different days, which is where a lot of the confusion starts. If you searched a market name and got hours that do not match the sign in front of you, you may be looking at a sibling market across town.

Hours usually fall between mid-morning and early afternoon, something like 8am to 1pm or 9am to 2pm. Vendors have been loading trucks since before dawn, so afternoons are for driving home. Evening markets exist too, often on a summer weeknight, running something like 4pm to 7pm.

Saturday morning is the classic slot and by far the most common one. If that is the morning you have free, you can [browse Saturday markets by state](/farmers-markets/saturday) and work from there.

## Winter Markets Are Their Own Thing

Plenty of markets that close outdoors come back in some reduced form for the cold months. These often move indoors to a school gym, a fairgrounds building, or a community center, and many switch from weekly to once or twice a month. Hours are usually shorter. The tables look different too, heavier on storage crops, meat, eggs, cheese, and bread, plus whatever greens a grower can pull out of a hoop house.

## The Same Market Can Have Two Schedules

Season boundaries are where listings go stale. A market might run 8am to 1pm every Saturday from June through October, then switch to 10am to 1pm on the first and third Saturday through March. Look up the hours in July, show up in December, and you can be an hour early to a market that is not meeting that week at all.

Holiday weeks are the other regular exception. When a market day lands on Thanksgiving, the Fourth of July, or a long weekend, organizers often shift to a different day, run a one-off holiday market, or skip the week.

## Check the Week You Plan to Go

Look up the market close to your visit, not months ahead. Our [directory of farmers markets](/) lists market days and hours alongside products, SNAP and EBT acceptance, and a link to the market's official site where one exists, built from USDA Local Food Portal data and government open-data portals. You can also compare [opening days and hours across the directory](/farmers-markets/hours) when you are lining up a few markets in one week.

For the exact date you are going, the market's own website or social account is the last word. That is where cancellations and season opening dates get posted first, usually days before anything else catches up.

## When to Arrive, and When Not to Bother

The first hour has the fullest tables. Berries, tender greens, and good bread go early, and so does anything a farmer only brought a few flats of. If there is one thing you came for, be there when it opens.

The last half hour is the other useful window. Some vendors would rather sell the end of a crate than load it back on the truck, so you may get a deal on what is left. Plenty of farmers hold their prices, though, and the selection is thinner, so treat it as a maybe rather than a plan.

Most markets run rain or shine, but policies differ. A steady drizzle usually means a smaller market with fewer vendors, not a closed one. Lightning or high wind can call it off outright, and that decision often gets made the morning of.
